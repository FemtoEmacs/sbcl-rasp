Medical Artificial Intelligence

Reports of the Abraham-Flexner Group

  • Grounding generative AI in the real world and objective reasoning: biological experiments, formal methods, and Schememax tools for testing medical AI.
  • Do generative AIs respect human priorities?: a document describing experiments that tested whether generative AI follows human requirements for efficiency and speed. Codex substituted bubble sort for quicksort and lists for arrays. Quicksort is generally faster than bubble sort. The document includes dated chat excerpts and records of its authorship.
  • Truth-resistant AI: autoclave bags, tests against reality, and the comparison between biohazards and AI hazards.
  • Medical Artificial Intelligence in Brazil and the United States, Bulletin Number One (October 2026). NAIA and OpenEvidence examined on a published paper; every answer checked against the paper's full text.
  • Medical Education in the United States and Canada: a digest of Flexner's 1910 report (cover, introduction, the two-year college requirement, ten school entries), with a thought experiment applying his headings to two schools of 2026.
  • sbcl-rasp: the tools used, the English parser RASP, the editor Browsered and its autoclave, and qprolog.
